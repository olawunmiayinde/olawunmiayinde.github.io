01 / About

Researching institutions, people, and the forces that shape opportunity.

I am a Ph.D. Candidate in Economics at the University of Utah. My research examines how institutions and historical legacies influence human capital, labor-market outcomes, health, inequality, and economic development.

My dissertation brings together development economics, labor economics, political economy, and economic history. I also work on health, caregiving, law and economics, and policy evaluation using administrative, survey, legal, and spatial data.

Before and alongside my academic work, I have built a career in risk, compliance, finance, strategy, and development across academic, multilateral, banking, consulting, and professional-services settings.

02 / Research

Research

My research program asks how institutions—historical and contemporary—shape economic opportunity, labor markets, health, and human capital.

Job Market Paper

The Long-Run Effects of Colonial Educational Legacy: Language, Migration, and Human Capital in Nigeria

Using a spatial regression discontinuity across historical colonial boundaries, this paper estimates the causal effects of English-language educational gatekeeping on human capital formation and high-skilled emigration. Results challenge “brain drain as gain” narratives by showing persistent colonial-era distortions in talent allocation and global labor market inequality.
