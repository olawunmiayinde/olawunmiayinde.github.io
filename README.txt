About Me

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Economics at the **University of Utah**, specializing in **Health Economics, Labor Economics, Development Economics, Welfare Economics, Public Economics, Law & Economics, and Political Economy.

My research examines how **economic vulnerability, shocks, caregiving responsibilities, mental health systems, financial conditions, and labor-market institutions** shape productivity, welfare, and population health.

My work integrates applied microeconometrics, macro-micro analysis, vulnerability measurement, welfare valuation, cost-of-illness estimation, and policy evaluation** using administrative health records, survey data, policy panels, longitudinal data, and time-series methods.

Job Market Papers

* Psychiatric Service Utilization, Vulnerability, and Economic Burden under Nigeria's Pre-2023 Mental Health System*

Using patient-level psychiatric records, macroeconomic indicators, and major crisis shocks—including the Global Financial Crisis, Ebola epidemic, and COVID-19 pandemic—this paper develops a **Mental Health Vulnerability Index (MHVI), estimates pathways of psychiatric service utilization, and quantifies productivity losses and the broader economic burden under Nigeria's pre-2023 mental health system.


* Measuring and Valuing Structural Caregiver Vulnerability in the United States: 

Development of a Caregiver Vulnerability Index (CVI), Leave-One-Out identification framework, and Marginal Compensation Rate (MCR)** to measure and value structural vulnerability among adult caregivers using BRFSS 2019-2022 dataset.

---

Working Papers & Research in Progress

* Labor Market Institutional Generosity, Economic Risk Protection, and Population Mental Health in the United States, 1999–2021: Examines how labor-market institutional generosity and economic risk protection relate to population mental health across U.S. states.

* How Worthy is Thy Skin Tone? A Socioeconomic Analysis in Nigeria: An empirical investigation of the socioeconomic consequences of skin tone and colorism in Nigeria.

* nGendered Crises: Economic Shocks, Epidemics, and Mental Health in Nigeria, 2000–2023: Studies gender differences in the relationship between major economic and epidemiological shocks and mental health outcomes in Nigeria.

*Labor Market Institutional Generosity and Birth Outcomes in the United States | with Megan Reynolds.

* Colonial Educational Legacy, Human Capital Formation, and Migration in Nigeria: Investigates the long-run effects of colonial educational institutions on human capital formation, migration, and the allocation of skilled workers in Nigeria.

* Fueling Inflation: PMS Pump Price Pass-Through to Staple Food Prices in Nigeria: Studies the transmission of Premium Motor Spirit (PMS) pump-price changes into staple food prices and household economic welfare.
